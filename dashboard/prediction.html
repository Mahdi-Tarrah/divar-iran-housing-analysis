Predicting rent and sale price

Two separate regression models, both XGBoost trained on the log of the price. The rules are the same for both: every feature and parameter choice is made on validation only, the test set is used once for the final model, and every group statistic is computed from the train split alone, so nothing leaks.

Together these raised validation R² from 0.591 to 0.612. city_type, luxury_score and has_cooling_system were tried as well, gave no gain, and were dropped.

Cleaning & target

  • Residential and commercial rent only. Daily/temporary rent is priced differently.
  • Rows without deposit or rent, and values of 100 billion or more (typos), are removed.
  • Implausible sizes are capped per property type (for example, apartments over 500 m²). A plain IQR would also remove genuinely large properties.
  • About 17% of ads have zero rent ("full deposit"). That is not a real rent, so these ads are removed from the target.
  • Rent is strongly skewed (median 6.3M, max 13B), so the model learns log1p(rent).
Caveat: the model uses the listing's own deposit as an input. This is not leakage, because the deposit is known before the rent, but a large part of the accuracy comes from this one column. Notebook 3 also trains a model without the deposit for comparison.

Leak-free imputation

  • The data is split before any preprocessing.
  • Each column with gaps has its own transformer, fitted on train only. Fill order: median/mode of cat3_slug → cat2_slug → city → global.
  • Rooms and amenities are filled by property type and size band (low < 150 m², medium < 300 m², high).
  • The bottom and top 5% of log price (zeros and typos) are removed. The metrics therefore apply to typical prices.
An MAE of about 1.36B against a median price of about 3B looks large, but the error is relative: small for cheap properties and large for expensive ones. The data mixes apartments, villas, land, pre-sale and commercial property across hundreds of cities, and lacks key information such as build quality.

What does the error mean for one listing?

The models are trained on log price, so the error is multiplicative: a log error of e means "off by a factor of exp(e)". Enter a price to see the typical band around it.
